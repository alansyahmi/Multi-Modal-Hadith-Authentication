TL;DR
Automated narrator evaluation is technically feasible using a mix of rule-based NER, statistical/sequence models, pretrained transformers, and knowledge-graph methods; recent datasets and KG resources enable disambiguation and graph-based verification but narrator-name variation and scarce labelled Jarh/Ta'dil labels remain core challenges.

NER and entity linking
This section summarizes the concrete NLP approaches applied to extract narrator names and link them to identities in Hadith chains, showing a progression from rule-based to transformer+KG methods. The literature reports hybrid pipelines combining linguistically driven rules, sequence models, and transformer fine-tuning plus dedicated linking into narrators knowledge graphs.

Datasets resources and practical inputs
This section lists the concrete datasets and corpora used by the reviewed studies and notes typical provenance used for experiments and evaluation. Several public and constructed datasets are in active use for extraction, disambiguation, and graph experiments.
1. (MDPI) AR‑Sanad 280K A 280K artificial sanads dataset with labelled narrator identities has been created and used as a primary benchmark for narrator disambiguation experiments
2. (ScienceDirect) Sanadset 650K: Data on Hadith narrators
3. Real canonical books Models trained/evaluated on AR‑Sanad have also been tested on real sanads drawn from the classical “six books” of hadith to measure real-world transfer
4. Sahih Muslim subsets Some extraction and processing studies used Sahih Muslim corpora for training/testing of Isnad POI extraction methods
5. Manually annotated corpora Prior work created manually annotated Hadith corpora for NER and converted extracted sequences to graph format for downstream analysis
6. Knowledge graph resources NarratorsKG and other KG-based indices are emerging as reusable resources to support entity linking and graph queries
