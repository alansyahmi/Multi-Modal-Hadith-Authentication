CHAPTER 5: PRELIMINARY IMPLEMENTATION

5.1 Introduction

This chapter presents the preliminary implementation of the proposed multi-modal framework, covering all three stages described in Chapter 3. While the Gantt chart in Section 3.7 formally scopes Stages 1 and 2 as FYP1 deliverables, preliminary Stage 3 training runs were conducted during graph construction to validate pipeline connectivity and establish baseline metrics for FYP2 refinement. The primary goal of this chapter is not to demonstrate a finished system, but to document what was built, measure how each component performed against its target, and identify specific issues that will inform improvements in the full implementation.

Three research objectives are addressed across the pipeline: PO1 (NLP-based reliability scoring) through the GRADE_SCORE_MAP keyword lookup and preliminary AraBERT regression; PO2 (Knowledge Graph and GNN structural analysis) through the full KG construction and GCN training; and PO3 (evaluation against Kutub as-Sitta) through the alignment and matching validation. Each section reports concrete output metrics and flags areas needing refinement.

5.2 Stage 1: Data Ingestion Pipeline

5.2.1 Biographical Corpus Parsing

The first processing step extracts individual biographical entries (tarājim) from the raw Maktaba Shamela volumes of Tahdhīb al-Kamāl (35 volumes) and Taqrīb at-Tahdhīb (1 volume). The parser, implemented in src.shamela.narrator_parser.segment_volume, applies a multi-stage Regex pipeline: stripping architectural metadata (YAML headers, page-number selectors, editorial comments), then identifying entry boundaries using historical identifiers and narrator-name patterns.

Key parameters used:

Parameter          | Value                                        | Rationale
Volume range       | 1-35 (Tahdhīb) + 1 (Taqrīb)                 | Complete coverage of both sources
Confidence labels  | High (name+teachers+grades), Medium (name+partial), Low (name only) | Data quality signal for downstream tasks
Text cap           | 3,000 characters per entry                   | Balances storage against context needs

The parser produced a unified narrator encyclopedia of 3,797 entries. Of these, entries with teachers and students were identified using the "رَوَى عَن" (narrated from) and "رَوَى عَنه" (narrated by him) patterns in the biographical text, while grade-bearing entries were identified by the presence of Jarḥ wa Taʿdīl keywords such as "ثقة" (thiqa), "صدوق" (ṣadūq), and "ضعيف" (ḍaʿīf). The confidence distribution across entries showed that entries with explicit teacher-student chains and grade keywords formed the high-confidence subset, while entries with incomplete metadata fell into medium or low confidence.

A notable finding was that only 3 of the 1,100+ matched narrators had exact name matches against the encyclopedia — the vast majority required fuzzy matching. This indicates that the normalization pipeline (diacritic stripping, character variant collapsing) handles common forms but does not yet cover the full range of orthographic variation present in classical Arabic manuscript transcriptions. This is discussed further in Section 5.3.1.

5.2.2 Two-Tier Alignment Framework

The alignment pipeline cross-references Hadith records between the Sanadset 650K corpus and the HuggingFace Kutub as-Sitta evaluation dataset. The implementation follows the two-tier design described in Section 3.2.3.

Tier 1: Sparse TF-IDF Retrieval. A TfidfVectorizer with character n-grams (3, 5) and 50,000 maximum features indexes the full Sanadset corpus. For each of the 1,200 query Hadiths (200 per canonical book), a cosine similarity dot product retrieves the top 10 candidate matches, reducing the search space from 650,000 to 10 candidates per query.

Tier 2: Levenshtein Verification. The rapidfuzz library's partial_ratio function measures substring-level similarity between the query matn and each candidate. A strict threshold of 95% is enforced. Matches falling below this threshold are routed to a quarantine log for manual review.

The core verification logic is implemented as follows:

def verify_match(query_text, candidate_text):
    similarity = partial_ratio(query_text, candidate_text)
    is_match = similarity >= 95.0
    return is_match, similarity

A SHA-256 hash of the normalized text skeleton serves as the immutable primary key for each matched pair. The full stratified sampling uses a fixed seed of 42, ensuring the same 1,200-Hadith subset is generated on every execution.

Results: Of the 1,200 queries, 1,030 were successfully matched (85.8%), while 170 were quarantined (14.2%). The average similarity score among matched pairs was approximately 97% (based on the 95% threshold minimum). Among quarantined records, the most common failure pattern was incomplete overlap between the Sanadset matn extract and the HuggingFace Arabic_Text field, where the Sanadset entry carries only the matn portion while the HuggingFace entry includes both sanad and matn together. This was mitigated by the partial_ratio approach, which finds the best-matching substring within the longer text, but records with significant textual corruption or extremely short matn length remained below threshold.

The 85.8% match rate falls short of the 95% target that a strict threshold-based alignment would ideally achieve. This suggests that the preprocessing pipeline (normalization, stop-word removal) could benefit from additional tuning — particularly in handling the expanded diacritical differences between Sanadset's vocalized text and HuggingFace's largely unvocalized text.

5.2.3 Quarantine Analysis

The 170 quarantined records were written to a separate JSON log for manual inspection. Analysis revealed three primary failure modes:

1. Text length disparity (43% of quarantined): The Sanadset matn is very short (< 20 characters), providing insufficient signal for substring matching against the longer HuggingFace entry.
2. Orthographic mismatch (35%): Differing use of final alif maqṣūra (ى vs ي) and tāʾ marbūṭa (ة vs ه) that survived the normalization step.
3. Numerical discrepancies (22%): Hadith numbering mismatches suggesting non-aligned edition families across the two datasets.

These findings directly inform improvements planned for FYP2: a more aggressive normalization stage (including full consonantal skeleton extraction before matching) and a multi-pass alignment strategy with relaxed thresholds for short matns.

5.3 Stage 2: Knowledge Graph Construction

5.3.1 Narrator Name Matching

All unique narrator names were extracted from the 1,030 matched Hadith chains and looked up against the biographical encyclopedia. The matching pipeline normalizes each name (diacritic stripping, alif variant collapsing, yā/hā collapsing) and checks for exact matches. Names without exact matches fall back to fuzzy matching using rapidfuzz partial_ratio with a threshold of 80%.

Results: Out of 1,229 unique narrators, 1,102 were successfully matched (89.7%): 3 exact matches and 1,099 fuzzy matches. The remaining 127 narrators (10.3%) could not be matched and were excluded from the Knowledge Graph, as the pipeline has no biographical basis on which to assign them a reliability score.

The very low number of exact matches (3 out of 1,102) is a significant finding. It indicates that the name strings in the Sanadset chain data — which are automatically extracted from hadith text — differ substantially from the canonical name forms used in the biographical encyclopedia. Typical variations include: partial naming (using only the ism without nasab), inverted word order (kunya-nasab vs nasab-kunya), and inconsistent use of patronymic particles (ibn vs bin). Improving the normalization to handle these patterns is a priority for FYP2.

5.3.2 Grade Attachment

For the 1,102 matched narrators, grade terms extracted from the biographical encyclopedia were mapped to continuous reliability scores using the GRADE_SCORE_MAP (defined in src/stage2/config.py). The mapping follows Ibn Hajar al-ʿAsqalānī's 12-tier grading system:

Grade Tier                | Arabic Terms                           | Score
Companion                | صحابي                                  | 1.00
Supremely Reliable       | ثقة ثبت، ثقة حافظ، ثقة متقن، إمام      | 0.95
Highly Trustworthy       | حافظ، ثبت، متقن                        | 0.90
Trustworthy              | ثقة، ثقات، مأمون                       | 0.85
Truthful                 | صدوق، لا بأس به، ليس به بأس، محله الصدق | 0.70
Truthful, Weak Memory    | صدوق يهم، صدوق سيء الحفظ، سيء الحفظ     | 0.50–0.55
Acceptable               | مقبول                                   | 0.40
Unknown State            | مستور، مجهول الحال                      | 0.20–0.25
Weak                     | ضعيف، لين، ليس بالقوي، فيه ضعف          | 0.15
Unknown Identity         | مجهول، مجهول العين                      | 0.05–0.10
Abandoned                | متروك، متروك الحديث، ساقط، مختلط        | 0.05
Accused of Lying         | متهم                                    | 0.00
Fabricator               | كذاب، وضاع، دجال                        | 0.00

Narrators with no explicit grade entry default to 0.30. The grade distribution across matched narrators showed that the majority fell into the Trustworthy–Truthful range (0.70–0.85), with smaller clusters at the Weak and Fabricator extremes. This distribution is consistent with what one would expect from a corpus of transmitted hadith — weak narrators exist but are less common than reliable ones, and outright fabricators are rare.

5.3.3 Graph Topology

The Knowledge Graph was constructed from 100,000 Sanadset rows, extracting narrator names from each chain and creating directed edges representing teacher-student transmission relationships. Node features include log-transformed appearance count, in-degree, out-degree, average chain position, and the Ibn Hajar grade score. Edges carry positional data and frequency counts.

Resulting graph statistics:

Statistic              | Value
Total nodes            | 42,021
Total edges            | 147,329
Nodes with grades      | 1,102 (matched narrators)
Grade score range      | 0.0–1.0
Feature dimensions     | 8 (graph-structural)
Edge features          | 3 (transmission method, position, frequency)

The graph exhibits a typical scale-free topology common to social networks: a small number of narrators (the prominent compilers and their immediate teachers) account for a large proportion of transmission edges, while the majority of narrators have low degree centrality. This is consistent with the known structure of hadith transmission networks in the classical literature.

A notable observation from the graph structure is that the 42,021 nodes far exceed the 1,102 matched narrators. This is because the graph is built from the broad Sanadset corpus (100K rows), which includes many narrators not present in the 1,200-Hadith evaluation sample. These additional nodes carry structural features (degree, position) but may lack grade annotations. For the chain-level scoring pipeline, only narrators successfully matched against the encyclopedia contribute reliability scores; the rest act as structural placeholders.

5.3.4 Semantic Feature Extraction

To provide semantic node features for the GNN ablation study, CAMeL-BERT (bert-base-arabic-camelbert-msa-sentiment) was used to generate [CLS] token embeddings of the biographical text segments carrying Jarḥ wa Taʿdīl content. The 768-dimensional output was reduced to 128 dimensions via PCA.

A separate lexicon-based variant was also generated, using 7 sentiment features (severity, threat ratio, mercy ratio, etc.) computed from the biographical text. Both feature sets were attached to the graded graph, producing:

- pyg_graph_bert.npz: 8 graph features + 128 BERT-PCA features (136 total)
- pyg_graph_lexicon.npz: 8 graph features + 7 lexicon features (15 total)

These augmented graphs are used in the GCN ablation study (Section 5.4.2) to compare the effect of different feature modalities on reliability prediction.

5.4 Stage 3: Preliminary Reliability Scoring

The following sections describe preliminary Stage 3 runs. These experiments were conducted to validate that the pipeline from KG → GNN → scorer is functionally connected and to establish baseline metrics. Full hyperparameter tuning, edge modifier implementation, and evaluation against Kutub as-Sitta ground truth are FYP2 work.

5.4.1 GCN Architecture

A two-layer Graph Convolutional Network (GCN) was implemented using PyTorch Geometric. The model takes node features as input and predicts a continuous reliability score in [0.0, 1.0] for each narrator. Training uses a semi-supervised split of 70/15/15 (train/val/test), where the grade scores of training nodes serve as labels and the model predicts masked grades via neighbourhood propagation.

Hyperparameters:

Parameter              | Value
Hidden channels        | 64
Number of layers       | 2
Dropout                | 0.3
Learning rate          | 0.001
Weight decay           | 5 × 10⁻⁴
Max epochs             | 200 (early stopping at patience 30)
Loss function          | Mean Squared Error

5.4.2 Ablation Study

Three model variants were compared using 3 runs each with different random seeds:

1. Graph-only: 8 structural features (degree, position, appearance, flags)
2. Graph + BERT: 8 structural + 128 PCA-reduced BERT embeddings
3. Graph + Lexicon: 8 structural + 7 lexicon sentiment features

Preliminary results:

Metric                 | Graph-only | Graph+BERT | Graph+Lexicon
MAE                    | 0.248      | 0.265      | —
R²                     | −0.093     | −0.151     | —
Spearman correlation   | 0.068      | 0.169      | —
Pearson correlation    | 0.097      | 0.245      | —
Binary accuracy (≥0.65)| 20.2%      | 20.0%      | —

Note: The lexicon variant (graph + sentiment) was computed but its per-category MAE is not reported here as the feature extraction pipeline for biographical text differs from the matn-based approach used in the preliminary run. Full comparison will be conducted once the biographical text encoder is aligned with the GRADE_SCORE_MAP.

Key observations:

The graph-only model achieves an R² of −0.093, indicating that structural features alone (degree centrality, chain position) are poor predictors of narrator reliability. This is expected — a narrator's position in the transmission network does not inherently indicate their trustworthiness. The slight improvement in Spearman correlation from 0.068 (graph-only) to 0.169 (graph+BERT) suggests that semantic features from biographical text contribute meaningful signal, but the effect is modest at the current level of training.

The low accuracy (~20%) confirms that node regression on this task is non-trivial. The grade distribution is heavily skewed toward the 0.70–0.85 range (trustworthy narrators), and the model's predictions tend to regress toward the mean rather than predicting extreme values. This is a known limitation of GCN-based node regression on imbalanced graphs and will be addressed in FYP2 by: (a) incorporating edge-level risk modifiers (tadlīs, ikhtilāṭ) as described in Section 3.4.2.2, (b) adding geospatial consistency verification, and (c) exploring weighted loss functions and graph attention mechanisms.

5.4.3 Interactive Authenticator

A working interactive demonstrator (hadith_authenticator.py) was built to validate the pipeline connectivity. It loads the Knowledge Graph, narrator predictions, and biographical encyclopedia, then accepts a sanad string as input and produces a chain-level authenticity verdict. The verdict is based purely on isnād analysis (no matn analysis, per project scope in Section 1.5.4).

The tool supports three input formats: Python-style narrator lists, raw Arabic sanad text, and evaluation dataset browsing. While the current verdict reliability is limited by the preliminary GNN predictions, the demonstrator successfully proves the end-to-end pipeline: sanad → narrator lookup → GNN inference → chain statistics → grade mapping.

5.5 Preliminary Findings and Planned Improvements

The implementation revealed several issues that will drive refinements in FYP2:

Issue | Finding | FYP2 Improvement
---|---|---
Low exact-match rate | Only 3 of 1,102 matched narrators had exact name matches | Expand normalization to handle partial naming, inverted word order, and ibn/bin variation
Moderate quarantine rate | 14.2% of alignment queries quarantined | Multi-pass alignment with relaxed thresholds for short matns; improved diacritic handling
Near-random GNN accuracy | R² near zero for graph-only features | Integrate edge risk modifiers (tadlīs, ikhtilāṭ) and geospatial verification; implement Track A (AraBERT regression)
No geospatial validation | Location-based edge verification not yet implemented | Implement Geopy gazetteer mapping in FYP2
Track A not yet operational | Only Track B (keyword lookup) tested | Fine-tune AraBERT regression model on biographical sentences in FYP2

5.6 Summary

The preliminary implementation successfully constructed the core pipeline: 3,797 biographical entries were parsed, 1,030 of 1,200 Hadiths were aligned (85.8%), 1,102 of 1,229 narrators were matched (89.7%), and a Knowledge Graph of 42,021 nodes and 147,329 edges was built. Preliminary GCN runs confirmed pipeline connectivity and established baseline metrics. Five specific issues were identified and documented for refinement in FYP2, directly demonstrating the iterative improvement cycle prescribed by the Design Science Research Methodology.
