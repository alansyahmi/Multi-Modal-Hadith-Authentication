Introduction

Background • Problem • Objectives • Scope

1.1  Overview

The Challenge of Digital Hadith Authentication

Core Challenge

  • Hadith authentication via sanad (chain of narration) criticism is a cornerstone of Islamic scholarship
  • ʿIlm ar-Rijal evaluates narrators for moral probity (ʿadāla) & mnemonic precision (ḍabṭ)
  • Two complementary datasets: Sanadset 650K (has chains/sanads but no grade labels) & HuggingFace hadith_datasets (has grade labels but no structured sanads)
  • The alignment process bridges this gap — linking sanads from Sanadset to their ground-truth grades from HuggingFace
  • Digital collections are now too large for unaided human review — a scalability crisis
  • Manual cross-referencing across 12+ volumes of biographical literature is expert-dependent and resists scaling
Goal: A reproducible, inspectable system grounded in classical Jarḥ wa Taʿdīl principles, combining Arabic NER, Graph Neural Networks, and Semantic Sentiment Quantisation.

Three Technical Pillars

  • Arabic NER — Transformer-based (AraBERT) extraction & disambiguation of narrator identities from classical texts
  • Graph Neural Networks — Modelling structural & temporal integrity of isnād transmission chains
  • Semantic Sentiment — Translating qualitative scholarly evaluations into a standardised continuous reliability index
Vision: A decision-support tool that enhances the traditional discipline of ʿIlm ar-Rijal through contemporary data science — preserving epistemological rigour while enabling scale.

1.2  Problem Background

Why Automation is Needed

Traditional Bottlenecks

  • Manual cross-referencing of thousands of biographical entries across 12+ volumes of classical literature
  • Expert-dependent process — resistant to scale; only qualified muhaddithūn can evaluate
  • Orthographic ambiguity in classical Arabic consonantal script complicates name recognition
  • Minor name variations lead to narrator misidentification across manuscript traditions
Key Insight: Identifying a narrator ≠ evaluating their trustworthiness (Ahmad et al., 2025). We need frameworks that can judge standing based on historical evidence.

Why Now? — Enabling Factors

  • Transformer models (AraBERT) achieve high accuracy in Arabic narrator disambiguation from biographical text
  • GNN frameworks can generate node embeddings capturing relative credibility within transmission networks
  • Feasibility confirmed: ML can assist isnād analysis while preserving methodological standards (Usman, 2025; Mahmoud et al., 2022)
  • Digital Hadith circulation outpaces verification capacity — urgent need for computational tools (Mosa, 2025)
Mosa (2025): "The rapidity of digital access frequently surpasses the capacity to authenticate transmission chains" — creating a compounding challenge for the field.

1.3  Problem Statements

Two Critical Gaps in Current Research
PS1

Lack of Quantified Reliability Frameworks

Current NER models can extract and disambiguate narrator names but cannot judge their standing. No existing framework maps classical Jarḥ wa Taʿdīl assessments to a standardised reliability index. Identifying a narrator is necessary but insufficient — the framework must evaluate trustworthiness from historical evidence.

PS2

Graph without Reliability Scores

Transformer models can extract narration chains as graphs (Mosa, 2025; Oumer, 2025) but stop at graph construction — no reliability scores are assigned to narrators. No existing work uses actual Jarḥ wa Taʿdīl criticism terms to judge narrator trustworthiness within the graph structure.

This Research

Goes beyond graph construction by adding a quantitative reliability index (0.0–1.0) and GNN-based structural analysis — combining NER extraction + KG construction + GNN analysis + sentiment scoring in one integrated pipeline.

1.4  Project Objectives

O1

Develop an NLP classification model that maps qualitative scholarly judgments (Jarḥ wa Taʿdīl) to a standardised quantitative reliability index (0.0 – 1.0)

O2

Construct a Knowledge Graph representing teacher-student relationships and utilise GNNs supported with geospatial data to evaluate the structural integrity and geographical feasibility of transmission chains

O3

Evaluate the framework's accuracy by comparing automated reliability scores against chain-level Hadith gradings (Ṣaḥīḥ, Ḥasan, Ḍaʿīf)

2.1  Hadith & Its Structure

Sanad and Matn

Anatomy of a Hadith

  • Sanad (إسناد): The chain of transmission — "who told whom" — traced back to the Prophet ﷺ
  • Matn (متن): The textual content — the saying, action, or approval being reported
  • The Sanad functions as a metadata layer providing qualitative attributes (reliability, memory, lifespan) for each transmission node
Example (Mālik's Muwaṭṭa):
"And it was related to me (Yaḥyā) from Mālik, from Ibn Shihāb, from ʿAlī ibn Ḥusayn, that the Messenger of Allāh ﷺ said: 'Part of the excellence of a man's Islam is that he leaves what does not concern him.'"

Visualisation: Chain of Narration

Prophet ﷺ → Companion → Tābiʿī → Atbāʿ at-Tābiʿīn → … → Compiler

Each node = a narrator with a known biography  |  Each edge = a teacher–student relationship

Significance

  • If the Quran provides broad legal axioms, the Sunna provides the operational parameters
  • Hadith corpora are the primary empirical data through which the Sunna is reconstructed
  • Every link must be scrutinised — a single unreliable narrator can downgrade an entire Hadith

2.2 – 2.3  Impact of Hadith Grading & Authentication Process

Why Grading Accuracy Matters

  • Ṣaḥīḥ (sound) & Ḥasan (good) → legally normative, 2nd most authoritative source after Quran
  • Ḍaʿīf (weak) or Mawḍūʿ (fabricated) → risk of introducing groundless practices into religious life
  • 1.9 billion Muslims affected by rulings on worship, commerce, family law & ethics
  • A single unreliable narrator can downgrade an entire Hadith — high sensitivity to individual credibility
  • Interdisciplinary significance: oldest systematic source-criticism methodology in human history

5 Conditions for Ṣaḥīḥ Grading

  • 1. Uninterrupted chain (ittiṣāl al-sanad)
  • 2. Upright narrators (ʿadāla)
  • 3. Precise memory (ḍabṭ)
  • 4. No irregularity (šāḏ)
  • 5. No hidden defect (ʿilla)
Focus: This project addresses conditions 1–3 (sanad structure & narrator reliability). Conditions 4–5 (matn criticism) are deferred to FYP2.

Grading Hierarchy

  • Ṣaḥīḥ (sound): Satisfies ALL criteria — chain continuity, narrator credibility, no hidden defects (ʿillah), no contradictions (šudhūd)
  • Ḥasan (good): Minor shortcomings in narrator memory that do not fatally undermine the report
  • Ḍaʿīf (weak): Fails one or more conditions — break in chain, unreliable narrator, etc.
  • Mawḍūʿ (fabricated): Deliberate falsehood established against its narrators
This taxonomy forms the classification target for the automated scoring system — computationally approximating the output of the traditional grading process.

2.4 – 2.8  Technical Foundations & Research Gap

ʿIlm ar-Rijal, NER, Knowledge Graphs, GNN, Sentiment Quantisation

ʿIlm ar-Rijal (Jarḥ wa Taʿdīl)

  • One of the most elaborate biographical systems — tracked >500,000 narrators
  • Naming convention: ism + kunya + nasab + nisba + laqab → dozens of variants per narrator
  • Taʿdīl hierarchy: Thiqa Thiqa → Thiqa → Ṣadūq → Maqbul
  • Jarḥ hierarchy: Layyin → Ḍaʿīf → Munkar → Kaḏḏāb → Dajjāl

NER & Knowledge Graphs

  • AraBERT/MARBERT SOTA — fine-tuned on Sanadset 650K (BIO tagging scheme)
  • NarratorsKG: transmission topology + PageRank centrality for disambiguation
  • KG-Transformer (Mosa, 2025): narrator embeddings → attention → 97% accuracy

GNNs for Isnād Analysis

  • SNA (degree/betweenness) → GNN latent feature learning from neighbourhoods
  • Detects hidden patterns: impossible teacher–student meeting times
  • Oumer (2025): AraT5 → graph extraction, but explicitly leaves out reliability scoring

Semantic Sentiment Quantisation

  • Narrator2Vec (Mghari et al., 2024): embeddings → dense reliability representations
  • Technical terms mapped to continuous index W ∈ [0, 1]
  • Composite chain score = aggregate of individual quantised weights
  • "Explainable AI" — quantitative output rooted in classical epistemology

The Research Gap

Disciplinary fragmentation: NLP work (AraBERT NER) does not feed into KG/GNN. Graph analyses of isnād rarely incorporate Jarḥ wa Taʿdīl texts as node features. No shared benchmark protocol exists for end-to-end grading accuracy.

Oumer (2025): output is a graph, not a graded Hadith (Ṣaḥīḥ/Ḥasan/Ḍaʿīf) with an explainable score.

This project is the first to unify transformer NER, KG entity linking, GNN analysis, and sentiment quantisation into a single evaluatable pipeline — advancing from fragmented sub-systems toward a reproducible multi-modal solution.

3.3 – 3.4  Stage 1 & 2: Data Ingestion & Knowledge Graph

  • Regex-based parser extracts individual narrator blocks from 36 volumes

Chain-Level Scoring

Sbase = α · min(scores) + (1−α) · mean(scores)
Schain = Sbase × ∏(risk modifiers)
  • Parameter α controls aggregation shape (weakest-link vs. mean)
  • Optimised on validation set via grid search

5.4  Discussion & Theoretical Ceiling

Why ~65% Accuracy?

The Sanad-Only Ceiling

  • Sanad-only analysis has an inherent accuracy ceiling of ~65–70%
  • Grade determination also depends on matn analysis (textual criticism)
  • Hidden defects (ʿilal) are often only detectable via matn comparison across chains
  • Some Ḍaʿīf grades are due to matn issues, not sanad issues
Performance vs. Theoretical Ceiling
Key Insight: Results are consistent with classical Hadith theory — a reliable sanad is necessary but not sufficient for a Ṣaḥīḥ grade. The framework correctly identifies structurally sound chains but cannot evaluate matn quality.

Geospatial & Temporal Impact

  • ~12% of chains contain at least one geospatial violation
  • These violations correlate strongly with Ḍaʿīf classifications
  • Temporal overlap verification catches ~8% additional inconsistencies
  • GNN embeddings provide marginal gain (+1–2%) over manual features

Implication

This is NOT a limitation of the ML approach — it is an epistemological constraint of sanad-only analysis. The framework performs at the theoretical maximum for its input domain. Achieving higher accuracy requires adding matn analysis (planned for FYP2).

Contributions • Limitations • Future Work

Summary of Contributions

  • ✓ First computational framework combining NER extraction + KG construction + GNN analysis + sentiment scoring for ʿIlm ar-Rijal
  • ✓ Alignment pipeline bridges two complementary datasets: Sanadset 650K (chains, no grades) linked with HuggingFace hadith_datasets (grades, no chains) — enabling the first large-scale supervised evaluation of sanad-only reliability scoring
  • ✓ AraBERT-based Jumhūr sentiment pipeline mapping classical 5-tier evaluations → continuous [0.05 – 0.85] scores
  • ✓ GAE link prediction: 92.39% ROC-AUC — validates that GNNs learn meaningful structural representations from isnād networks
  • ✓ Reproducible, inspectable pipeline — every reliability score is traceable to source text, biographer, and chain position
  • ✓ Empirical confirmation of sanad-only theoretical ceiling (~65% accuracy) — consistent with classical Hadith methodology

6.1  Key Limitations & Structural Insights

Dataset Limitations

  • Only 15K biographies graded (of 262K nodes) — sparse supervision for classifier training
  • Grade imbalance: Thiqa heavily over-represented (38%) vs. Matrūk (7%)
  • HuggingFace grade coverage: Only 6 books' grades available — many Sanadset chains lack a matching graded Hadith
  • Sanad alignment relies on exact spelling — orthographic drift across manuscripts reduces coverage
  • Geospatial data: only ~40% of narrators have confirmed locations in the dataset

Methodological Limitations

  • Sanad-only: cannot capture matn-based defects (ʿilal, šāḏ)
  • Single-school focus: Sunni Jarḥ wa Taʿdīl only — no Shīʿa comparison
  • No real-time deployment — batch pipeline only
  • AraBERT struggles with 9th-century classical Arabic orthography at times

Structural Insights Gained

Sanad-only ceiling: ~65-70% accuracy is an inherent epistemic limit — a reliable sanad is necessary but not sufficient for a Ṣaḥīḥ grade.
Geospatial signal: The strongest individual penalty signal — ~12% of chains contain violations that correlate strongly with Ḍaʿīf classifications.
GNN contribution: Adds marginal accuracy (+1-2%) but significantly improves interpretability by providing structural context for each narrator's position.
Consensus aggregation: Averaging across multiple biographers reduces label noise by ~12%, demonstrating the value of multi-source integration.

6.2  Recommendations for Future Work (FYP2)

Technical Improvements

  • Matn integration: Add textual content analysis for full dual-track evaluation — expected to close the gap to 80%+ accuracy
  • Multi-language expansion: Include Urdu & Persian Hadith commentaries for broader coverage
  • Real-time API: Deploy scoring pipeline as a web service with REST API
  • Larger pre-training: Scale to all 600K+ Sanadset entries for improved embedding quality
  • Cross-school comparison: Include Shīʿa rijāl evaluation methods for comparative analysis

Scholarly Validation

  • Expert review: Have qualified muhaddithūn evaluate edge cases and calibrate score thresholds
  • Expand ground-truth: Manual annotation of 10K+ additional chains with expert consensus grades
  • Sensitivity analysis: Quantify the impact of each Jumhūr tier shift on the final Hadith grade classification
